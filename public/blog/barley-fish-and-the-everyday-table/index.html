Everyday life

Barley, Fish and the Everyday Table

By T.R. Pomeroy · 2 min read · November 24, 2026

Away from the feast, the everyday meal tells a different story about life in the North.

Now if you could travel back and sit at a Viking table, there is one thing you would often see: Barley. Barley wasn’t just a crop—it was a backbone of Scandinavian survival. It grew faster than other grains, tolerated poor soil, and could withstand the shorter growing seasons of the north.

From barley came: Thick porridge. Rough flatbreads, cooked on stones or iron plates. Dense, dark loaves of bread.

In some regions, especially farther south or east, you’d also find: Oats. Rye. Barley was a foundation of the Viking Age diet. And it suits them perfectly—this wasn’t soft, fluffy bread like we have today. This was dense. Tough and gritty.

One way to preserve fish was stockfish. Stockfish was cod, dried in cold air until it became hard as wood. And here’s what makes it remarkable: It could last a long time. It didn’t spoil easily. It could be eaten as-is like a fish jerky or rehydrated to have a softer texture.

Vegetables don’t get much glory or attention in Viking stories…kind of like when we were kids, but they were essential. You’d find: Turnips. Cabbage. Onions. And leeks.

Before you think of all their dishes as boring and bland, realize they had herbs to add a little flavor. They weren’t necessarily seasoning like modern chefs or grandma—but they did use what they had: Dill. Thyme. Angelica.

Historical context & sources

Food varied by region, season and household. This excerpt focuses on staples; it is not a universal menu. Archaeological evidence also includes meat, fruit and dairy, and wheat was cultivated alongside barley, rye and other grains. [1] [2] [3]
