Hávamál: Something You Carry

By T.R. Pomeroy · 2 min read · November 24, 2026

Short observations, warnings and principles—and the question of what we do with them.

The Hávamál. “The Sayings of the High One.” Its speaking voice is associated with Odin.

And that title alone should make you pause. Because this isn’t advice from a king. Or a warrior. This is wisdom attributed to a god who sacrificed for knowledge—and then chose to pass it on.

But here’s where it gets interesting. The Hávamál isn’t written like a story. There’s no hero’s journey. No rising action. No final battle. Instead… It reads like fragments. Observations. Warnings. Principles. Almost like you’re overhearing the distilled thoughts of someone who has seen too much… and learned what actually matters. And scattered throughout it are lines that feel almost out of place in their simplicity. Short. Direct. Unapologetic. But behind each one… is a lifetime of experience.

Now here’s the thing. Most people read the Hávamál like poetry. They admire it. Maybe quote it. And then move on. But that misses the point entirely. Because this wasn’t written to be admired. It was written to be used.

And if you listen closely… You’ll start to notice something. This isn’t just ancient wisdom. It’s disturbingly relevant. Because even now— People still: say more than they should. Trust faster than they should. Neglect the relationships that matter. Chase things that don’t last. And stay where it’s comfortable… until it costs them. Different world. Same human nature. And that’s why this matters.

Historical context & sources

Hávamál is a composite wisdom poem preserved in the medieval Poetic Edda. This is T.R. Pomeroy’s personal reading of its practical appeal, not a translation of its verses or evidence that all Norse people understood it identically. [1]
