Odin at Mímir’s Well

By T.R. Pomeroy · 4 min read · November 24, 2026

The water waits. So does the price.

Odin has reached Mímir’s well beneath the world tree, seeking wisdom. The eye offered for a drink is a traditional mythic motif; the scene, dialogue and inward experience are the author’s creative retelling.

Mímir spoke. “You seek the wisdom of the well.” Not a question. A statement, a verdict. Silence added to the weight of the air. Odin didn’t answer, because what do you say… when someone exposes the deepest part of you? Mímir turns, just slightly, and gestures toward the well. The water does not ripple. It does not invite. It simply waits… for the one who is determined to drink.

Mímir says, “Drink.” The seeker, slightly taken aback by the opportunity, steps forward. The roots almost seem to twist and tighten in anticipation. The air grows heavy, and the vibration returns— as if the world itself feels the weight of this moment, like it’s a hinge in time. As Odin peered down at the non-reflective pool, Mímir lifts his hand. “To drink from the well will cost you.”

“No one drinks from my well for free.” The words hover for a moment. But the traveler did not hesitate. Because somewhere, deep down… He already knew that this pursuit would, in fact, cost him something. Mímir steps closer to Odin. Close enough that the truth in his gaze becomes unbearable. And the innermost thoughts are laid bare. Mímir is close enough to see everything that he is—

“It will cost you something that you can’t take back, there will be pain, there will be sacrifice, and there will be a change in your perspective.” “And the scar will be a reminder that the price was paid, so that no one will doubt that wisdom is embodied within you.” Then—quietly—with a whisper, he speaks the words that echo through all the realms and every saga that follows:

“It will cost you your EYE.”

The water isn’t free; the cost is one eye from the one who desires a drink. Odin steps forward. No hesitation. Only certainty. Because some decisions are not made in the moment— And some are made before the question is even asked. He kneels at the edge of Mímir's Well. Up close, the water remains black and still, like this is the only world where it could ever belong, because in Midgard or Asgard it wouldn’t make sense.

It’s as if this water has memory, understanding like it’s the kind of water that if you drink from it once, you’ll never have to drink from it again, not because you know everything, but because you understand what to do and where to go when wisdom or knowledge is lacking in the future. He leans in— and for a brief moment, he sees himself. Not a reflection of who he is, but a future version of what he could be.

And then— He lets go of who he was and embraces a future version that has less vision than before, but sees more clearly than ever. The offering is made. No spectacle. No cry. Just the quiet surrender of something that can never be returned. The eye falls— and the well accepts it. Mímir watches. Not surprised. He is never surprised. And then— he nods.

Permission. Odin cups the water in his hands. It is colder than ice—blacker than night. He drinks. And the world… changes. Not around him. Within him. The roots are no longer just roots— They are pathways. They are connected, they all originate in the same place, yet have great distinction when they break the surface. He now sees how one choice bends another. That all decisions matter. There’s no such thing as a big choice or a small one; they are all significant. Because in the end, there are only two paths, there have only been two paths, one path is truth, and the other is not.

How a single word is like a single spark, and even the smallest spark can set a whole forest on fire. The wisdom that unfolds comes fast and furious. If this were anyone else, it might leave them crippled by the weight of knowing and understanding. Momentarily on one knee, when he rose, he was something new.

Now standing the same height, but taller. The same mind but wiser. One eye—but with sharper vision than ever.
