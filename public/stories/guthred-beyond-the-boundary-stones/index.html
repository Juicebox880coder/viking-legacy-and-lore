Guthred: Beyond the Boundary Stones

By T.R. Pomeroy · 3 min read · November 26, 2026

The elder is missing. Guthred follows the tracks beyond the place where the village stops searching.

An excerpt from T.R. Pomeroy’s original supernatural fiction in the werewolf episode. Guthred has found a torn scrap of the elder’s clothing in the woods. The characters, Wyrdwood and events are fictional; this is not a translated saga or an account of a documented Viking incident.

They found the elder’s door ajar. Unlatched from within. His hearth had gone cold. His boots were still by the fire, dry and waiting. A mug of untouched ale trembled slightly on the table, as if disturbed… recently. But there was no sign of Haesten. Not in the longhouse. Not at the forge. Not even at the gravesite of his son, where he was often found at dusk, speaking to no one. By twilight, half the village was searching. And by nightfall, no one was speaking above a whisper.

Guthred returned to the woods. Not because he wanted to. Because he had to know. The elder had always been something more than a man—steel-backed, stone-faced. He was the kind of person whose presence anchored the world around him. But now… the world felt unmoored. The trees felt closer. The stars more distant. And every sound—snap of a twig, groan of a tree—seemed to breathe behind his neck.

Still, Guthred pressed deeper into the dark. He passed the old boundary stones—markers set generations ago, warning travelers not to go farther unless they sought to join the dead. Beyond them lay the Wyrdwood. It wasn’t the name that scared people. It was the stories. Trees that bled. Echoes that answered back. Shadows that moved when the wind didn’t. And worse: curses. The kind you didn’t bring back to the village. The kind you brought back inside you.

Snow cracked underfoot as Guthred followed the trail—bare feet, deep-gouged paw marks, zig-zagging like a beast that couldn’t decide whether it was crawling or marching. The prints led him to an outcrop overlooking the fjord. That’s where he saw it. Something kneeling in the snow. Naked. Hunched. Shuddering. The air stank—iron and rot. Guthred stepped closer, spear in hand. “Elder?” he called, though he already knew the answer. The shape flinched. Twitched. Then turned.

What looked back at him was not a man. Its jaw was too long. Its teeth too sharp. Its shoulders twitched as if the flesh no longer fit right. But the worst part? Its eyes. Not yellow. Blue. Haesten’s eyes. They flickered with a plea—just for a moment. Then something changed. The face twisted. The hands clenched into claws. And it shrieked—not like a wolf—but like a man being devoured by one.

Guthred didn’t run. Not yet. He stepped back, slow. The beast rose to two feet. Still human in shape. But wrong in every way. Taller. Joints bent oddly. Skin stretched too thin over sinew that pulsed like ropes. It stalked forward. One step. Then two. Then froze. It sniffed. Eyes narrowed. And then, impossibly, it spoke. Not in words. In Haesten’s voice—rasping, broken, echoing like it came from deep inside a cave.

“I tried… to take it off.” Then the creature lunged.

Guthred dove sideways, rolling through snow and pine needles. His shoulder slammed into stone. The spear skidded into the dark. Behind him—the sound of claws on bark, something massive leaping into the trees. Then silence. Not absence-of-noise silence. Hunting silence. The kind where even your own breathing betrays you.

He held still. Frozen. Listening. Branches creaked. Snow fell. A growl, low and wet, rose behind him— Then stopped. He turned slowly. Nothing. Only the wind. Only— Wait.

Footprints. Leading away. But not from the beast. From someone else. Booted. Human. Deliberate. Trailing around the scene. As if watching. As if following both hunter and prey. And left behind… a pendant. Hung from a strip of black leather. Etched with a rune Guthred didn’t recognize—twisted, tangled. Neither Norse nor Christian.

He picked it up. And from behind him, a voice spoke—low, female, calm as snowfall. “You shouldn’t have come here alone.”
