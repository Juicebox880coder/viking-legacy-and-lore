Aksel: The Price of Silence

By T.R. Pomeroy · 4 min read · November 24, 2026

His sister knows there is more to tell. Aksel will not let her say it.

Aksel has killed the chieftain’s son. Now his family stands before the assembly. This is an original fictional judgment scene, not a reconstruction of a documented trial; the hall, ritual and threats serve the drama.

The Lawspeaker’s voice fell silent, and the hall held its breath. The villagers stood still, as if motion might be mistaken for sympathy. No one dared blink. This was not the time for mercy. It was time for justice and judgment. The elders shifted beneath their heavy cloaks—old hands clenched over walking staves, eyes narrow with pain and pride. These were the men who had helped teach the chieftain’s son to ride, to hunt, to throw a spear, who had been present at his birth, who had once raised a horn to the young man proclaiming him as the future of their clan.

Now, they could barely look at the bound man in the center of the hall, who had stolen away their hopes and dreams. Aksel’s mother stood near the edge of the gathering, half hidden in the folds of the crowd, her face pale and frozen. Her eyes briefly glanced toward her son. Then she looked down. His father didn’t look at him at all. He stood at the back, head lowered, as if bowed beneath the weight of his offering—the silver, the sheep, the family legacy poured out like water into sand. Resigned to living out his days, poor, ashamed, and marginalized by his village for an act that his son committed.

The tension in the room was building as the verdict was just moments away. Solveig, his sister, barely seventeen, stepped out from the crowd. She had been crying. Her hands trembled as she stood alone. Her voice cracked as she said, “He didn’t start it.” A murmur rippled through the room like a warning. “My brother was—” “No!” Aksel said sharply. His voice cut through the air like an axe head. Calm. Direct. Final.

Solveig froze. He turned his head just slightly toward her. Their eyes met. And in that moment, she saw it—he wasn’t trying to silence her. He was trying to save her. If she spoke against the chieftain’s son—even dead—the cost would not stop with him. So she swallowed her words, tears returned, sliding down her cheeks as if mourning the truth that would never be known.

Aksel looked back to the Lawspeaker. “I regret nothing,” he repeated. The staff of judgment was raised. “By witness and ritual, by memory and oath, let it be known—Aksel Alrikson is declared útlagr. Outlaw. Outside the protection of our people, our gods, and our law. From this moment forward, anyone who aids this útlagr will share his fate.” A ripple spread through the crowd like ice cracking on a lake.

There were no shackles to drag him away. No blade would fall. Instead, the guards walked him to the edge of the village just as the sun began to rise behind them. They took nothing from him, because he owned nothing now—not even his name. By law, no one could legally feed him. No one could offer shelter. He could be killed on sight, and no blood price would be owed. He was dead, in the eyes of the law—but cursed to live it out in the cold.

The guards stood watch until Aksel disappeared over the hill where it dipped just before turning into the western forest. Solveig had left the hall before the elders had spoken. She ran home and then continued to the boundary stone at the treeline. She quietly sought her brother’s attention, not knowing if anyone would be following close behind. She was breaking the only rule for interacting with an outlaw: don’t. Breathless. Pale. And full of concern.

She pressed something into his hands. It was a pouch. Inside: flint, a bit of dried meat, and two silver coins. “You can’t go with nothing,” she whispered. “And here, I brought you your hunting knife.” He took the pouch and the knife. Then the two embraced. He kissed her on the forehead. “Do not speak of what happened that night to anyone,” he said. “Let them forget all about me.”

She shook her head, knowing that her safety was tied to her silence and her brother’s sacrifice. “You are not a murderer,” she whispered. “You’re my brother, and you have always been my shield.” But Aksel had already turned.
