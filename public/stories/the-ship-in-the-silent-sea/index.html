The Ship in the Silent Sea

By T.R. Pomeroy · 4 min read · November 24, 2026

Something scratches beneath the hull. Then the fog begins to move.

Einar’s crew encounters a ship in the fog north of Iceland. This original horror scene draws on Naglfar, the nail-ship of Norse myth. Einar, his brother and this encounter are invented, rather than an episode from the Eddas.

The sea went silent. No wind, no waves. Just silence. This wasn’t normal. A calm sea still breathes. It’s never completely still. Beneath the oars, a smooth reflective surface. Like the ship was floating on a lake of black water. The sea was holding its breath. The ship drifted beneath a moonless sky. The clouds were thick, deepening the darkness. Faint hues of green gave the sky a sick feeling, and the dark waters reflected it back.

The ship was somewhere north of Iceland, though no one aboard could say exactly where. Thick walls of gray surrounded them. A wet, suffocating fog was beginning to press in. No one moved in the silence. No gulls circled overhead. The oars rested above the water. The men were frozen along the benches, listening. A slow drip from the oars was the only sound and the only movement in the water.

Einar tightened his grip around the edge of the ship. His knuckles cracked pale in the dark. He had crossed storms that split ships in half. Raided monasteries while arrows fell like rain. Once fought a man with his own severed ear still hanging from his beard. But this? This made his stomach feel hollow. Because sailors knew things. And one of those things was this:

When the sea goes quiet… something is listening. A young crewman near the mast swallowed hard. “Maybe we should row.” No one answered him. Another sailor slowly made the sign of Thor’s hammer across his chest. Then came the sound. Scratch. Every head turned. Scratch… scratch… It came from beneath the hull. Like fingernails dragging slowly across wood. Not claws. Human nails. Long. Deliberate. Curious.

The youngest man aboard let out a shaky breath. “Did you hear that?” “Yes,” whispered another. “Don’t speak loud.” “Why?” The older sailor stared into the fog. His voice dropped low enough that the dark itself had to lean closer to hear him. “Because the dead travel easiest on calm seas.” Nobody laughed. Not one man. Because in the North, the dead were never far away.

They waited in burial mounds beneath the earth. They sat beside their treasure in the dark. They walked back to farms if buried wrong. They scratched at doors at night. They stood dripping beside beds. And sometimes… Sometimes they sailed. Then the fog moved. Not naturally. It peeled apart slowly ahead of them like wet skin separating over a wound. And there… A ship emerged.

Silent. Black water rolled beneath it, but no oars touched the sea. No sail pulled wind. No lanterns burned. It simply glided forward through the fog with the dreadful patience of something that already knew where it was going. Einar felt the hairs rise along his arms. The vessel was enormous. Far larger than any trading ship. Its mast leaned crooked against the sky like a gallows tree. Ropes hung limp and tangled, swaying slightly though there was no breeze.

Then lightning flickered somewhere inside the clouds. For one brief heartbeat, the world turned white. And they saw the hull. Not wood. Not entirely. Something pale covered it in overlapping layers. Curved pieces fitted together tightly like fish scales. Thousands of them. No… Not scales. Nails. Human fingernails. Yellowed. Cracked. Rotting. Hammered together into the sides of the ship. The lightning vanished. Darkness crashed back over the sea.

Several men began muttering prayers at once. One sailor fell to his knees. Another quietly started crying. Then came movement along the railing of the ghost ship. Figures rising slowly in the dark. Too slowly. Like drowned bodies remembering how joints worked. Water poured from their clothes. From their mouths. From the empty black pits where their eyes should have been. Einar couldn’t breathe. Because one of them stood near the front of the ship wearing a cloak fastened with a silver wolf clasp.

A clasp Einar himself had carved years ago. For his brother. His brother whose body had never been recovered from the sea. The dead man lifted his head. And smiled. A wet smile. Rotting lips peeling back slowly. Then the figure raised one swollen hand and pointed directly at Einar. The scratching beneath the hull returned. Louder now. Closer. Scratch. Scratch. Scratch. As though something beneath the ship was trying very hard…

To climb aboard.
