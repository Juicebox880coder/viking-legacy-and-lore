Eirik: The Day the Sun Darkened

By T.R. Pomeroy · 3 min read · November 27, 2026

Harvest is under way when the light begins to fail. Eirik gathers his family and waits.

An imagined Viking-age scene by T.R. Pomeroy. Eirik has returned from a raid and seen strange lights in the sky; now his family faces an eclipse during harvest. The characters, sequence of omens and reactions are fictional, not a documented eclipse or proof of a shared Norse interpretation.

Harvest finally came. Eirik and the family all had their assignments. Blades to cut. Bundles to be gathered. Normal chores that still needed to be done. And of course the food to fuel all of this effort needed to be prepared. Life pushed forward the way it always does—regardless of what the sky might have said.

Eirik worked the field with the others, sun high above. Warm. Steady. Reliable. This was Eirik’s favorite time of year. Blue skies, warm sun, and good honest, safe work. Until— Something changed. Not suddenly. Not violently. But something wasn’t quite right.

The light in the field began to dim. At first he thought it was nothing. A cloud floating by providing temporary shade. But with a quick glance upward, he saw there were no clouds in the sky today. Eirik paused. Others kept working. He stopped them, and said, “Do you see that?” Then all looked up. Eyes adjusting to staring at the sun, but then some said, “I see it. The edge of the sun is dark.” Then one by one everyone began to see it.

They continued to stare because the darkness was growing on the sun and the light all around them was becoming more dim. Even the temperature of the air cooled. Eirik was witnessing something he had never seen, never heard of, except from an old tale about a wolf that would devour the sun one day. And his chest tightened. By now the sun looked exactly like someone, or something, had taken a bite out of it. But it kept growing.

No one spoke. No one needed to. Because everyone had heard the story. No one spoke, but all Eirik could think was, “It’s happening.” The darkness grew. The field fell silent. Birds stopped singing. Then the night insects began their chorus of noise. The sun was gone, consumed, a black circle in the sky, with only the faintest edges of light that remained.

Eirik gathered his family together, arms around them. With the only thought that, if this is how it ends, if the land is plunged into the sea, then they will go out together as one. They held each other for what seemed like eternity. They stared, they listened, they sensed what was next.

Then— A sliver of light appeared. Small, growing. Returning. The darkness that covered the light of the world for a moment was being pushed back, rolled away to reveal the sun once more. The darkness retreated. The world breathed again. Sound returned, birds sang. Warmth followed. Life resumed.

But Eirik didn’t move because he was not sure what to think. So many signs in such a short time. What does it all mean?

Eirik returned to work his fields. He still sailed when he had to. Still lived as the men of the North do. But every morning— When the sun rose— He watched it. And he wondered, and pondered the meaning of what the sky was trying to say.
