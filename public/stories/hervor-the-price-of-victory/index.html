Hervor: The Price of Victory

By T.R. Pomeroy · 4 min read · November 24, 2026

The enemy runs. The battle is over. But Tyrfing is not done.

Hervor and Tyrfing come from Hervarar saga. This scene is the author’s imagined continuation: its battles, dialogue and sequence are creative storytelling, not a translation of the medieval saga.

Eistland was only the beginning. At first, the sword seemed to justify everything. A coastal ruler hired Hervor and her men to hunt raiders who had been burning villages and choking trade along the Baltic coast. Hervor met them on a wet black beach with grey skies and a mist in the air. When Tyrfing came free from its sheath, the battle ended almost before it began.

She and the sword moved like they already knew where men would die. She felt like a Valkyrie in that moment, choosing who would live and who would die in battle; in this case, everyone died. Word spread fast about Hervor and her army. Enemies who stood across the battlefield from Hervor began to hesitate; some still attacked, but many tried to flee. They won countless victories.

Not barely. But completely. Silver poured into their hands. More warriors joined their ship. Stories spread across the Baltic of a woman carrying the blade of the dead. Victory came too easily. And that is what should have frightened them. But success has a way of ignoring caution. Soon rulers who once would have dismissed Hervor now welcomed her into their halls. A powerful lord from the southern Baltic coast sent for her personally, offering silver, land, and reputation if she would crush his enemies. Kings who would never have listened to her before now poured her wine like she was the queen.

It was all because of the sword that once horrified them when it was buried on the island but now had gained them all more riches than they could have imagined. And slowly, dangerously, the crew stopped speaking of curses. Or the original tragedy. Years passed like that. Battles. Raids. One ship turned into a fleet. They all returned heavier with silver each trip, and Hervor herself began to change. She no longer carried Tyrfing like a burden. She wore it like proof that she had been right all along.

Maybe the sword had chosen her. Maybe weaker men had simply failed to master it. Maybe power only destroys those too weak to carry it. And that is the lie that gets whispered before the payment comes due. The first hint that something wasn’t right came during a winter battle along the frozen coastline north of Eistland. Hervor’s army arrived. Sword in hand. Everyone marched with the highest level of confidence, knowing the outcome would be the same.

But the enemy was aware of the outcome as well and instead of finding out if they were correct they fled. The line didn’t break, it never formed. The troops across the field from Hervor and her men left the moment they saw her and the sword in the distance. The battle was over before it started. The spoils of victory without even breaking a sweat.

But the sword was not done. Hervor tried to sheath Tyrfing. It would not allow it. Her breathing tightened. Not again. Not here. Around her, her men celebrated victory, unaware of the panic rising in her chest. The blade seemed almost alive in her hand now, trembling slightly as though demanding something unfinished. Blood. It always requires blood. One of her trusted warriors approached smiling, snow crunching beneath his boots as he moved toward her.

And for the first time since the haunted island… Hervor feared the thing in her hand. Because in that moment she understood. The curse was real and it had never left. Whether it was the sword or Hervor, no one knows for sure, but her soldier fell there in the snow. The weight of the action crushed Hervor, she sheathed the sword, dropped to her knees and wept for her fallen companion and friend.

“It wasn’t supposed to be like this,” she said with a tremble in her voice. The men stood frozen around her. Snow drifted through the silence. The battle cries were gone now. The wind blew across, stirring up snow dust. Hervor stared down at her companion lying motionless. This was no frightened deckhand standing near the prow of a ship. This was one of her warriors. A man who had fought beside her for years. A man who had trusted her enough to follow her into battle after battle.

And Tyrfing had taken him anyway. That night, no songs were sung, no toasts, just men speaking in whispers. Some blamed the sword. Others blamed the Norns. A few quietly blamed Hervor herself. Hervor sat apart from them all. Distant and distracted by the replay of the moment. Before dawn came, Hervor made a vow. No more. No more battles with this sword. The hardest part wasn’t putting the sword away.

It was feeling like she had become weaker without it. No more boldness, success no longer a given, and reputation fading to normal. And the crew noticed. Only the most loyal stayed, others left to find opportunity elsewhere.
