Erik: The Sea of Exile

By T.R. Pomeroy · 3 min read · November 24, 2026

A boy leaves Norway beside a father who refuses to bend.

After Thorvald’s exile from Norway, young Erik sails toward Iceland with his family. This is an imagined scene around saga tradition; the conversation and Erik’s inner thoughts are the author’s invention.

The sea did not welcome them. But it hadn’t rejected them either. The knarr groaned as it cut through black water, its hull creaking with every swell. The wind came in sideways, cold and constant, hurling mist and salt that stung the eyes. Every rope was damp. Every plank was slick. There was no escaping the damp cold. Even the air felt thick, like the clouds might descend upon them and swallow them whole.

Erik sat near the bow, knees pulled in, arms wrapped tight around them. He was cold, but there was a fire growing inside. He learned long ago that tears didn’t change anything. And that people respected, even feared, the flame. Thorvald stood at the stern. Always standing. Erik watched him more than he watched the sea. He studied the way his father held himself. Unbending.

Unapologetic. As if exile wasn’t a punishment… but an opportunity to prove the world wrong. As if the land they had just left behind had made a mistake. That night, the waves grew taller and more violent. Erik couldn’t sleep. The cold made sure of that. So he climbed to his feet and moved carefully across the deck, steadying himself against the rail. He made his way toward the stern, toward the only place that seemed anchored in something certain.

Thorvald didn’t turn when Erik approached. Erik was used to the cold from his father, even though he longed for attention. Something he would never get. “Can we go back?” Erik asked. The words felt small the moment they left his mouth. Thorvald’s eyes fixed on the boy as if he had just died on the battlefield because he was weak-minded. “No.” That was it.

No explanation. No words of wisdom to follow. Erik said with the faintest flicker of fire in his voice. “That was our home.” Thorvald shifted his gaze, just enough to look down at the boy. “It was land, just dirt,” he said. “Land can be taken. Land can be lost. And the land doesn’t care one way or another.” Thorvald turned fully and continued. With no regret, he spoke,

“You only have what you are willing to take… and what you are strong enough to keep.” Erik took it all in. Still disappointed about leaving, he now had a new vision that would guide him through similar circumstances just over two decades later. Days on the ship passed. Or maybe it was weeks. Time blurred when the horizon looked the same day in and day out.

Endless gray sky. Dark gray water. And an uncertain future. Until one morning, someone shouted. “Land!” It rose out of the mist and water like something untouched, untethered from the world. Erik and his family sensed a fresh start was waiting for them.
