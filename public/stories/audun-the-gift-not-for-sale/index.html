Auðun: The Gift Not for Sale

By T.R. Pomeroy · 2 min read · November 24, 2026

A poor traveler, a white bear, and a king offering the easy road home.

Auðun has brought a polar bear from Greenland and means to carry it onward as a royal gift. This excerpt is a creative retelling inspired by Auðunar þáttr vestfirzka; its dialogue is the author’s, not a quotation from a translation.

Before long, Auðun found himself standing a great distance from the harbor. Then a presence entered the room that felt every bit as large as the bear. It was the king. King Harald sat with the ease of a man confident and accustomed to being obeyed. He did not need to raise his voice. He did not need to lean forward. His eyes rested on the bear first.

Then on Auðun. “So,” Harald said, his tone measured, “you have brought something rare into my kingdom.”

“Yes,” Auðun answered.

“And you intend to take it with you from here?”

“Yes.”

A flicker of something passed through the king’s expression. Amusement, perhaps. Or disbelief. “Most men,” Harald said, “would consider it an honor to present such a gift here.”

“I have no doubt,” Auðun replied.

“Yet you choose to keep the beast.”

“Yes sir, I do.”

The room grew quieter. Harald leaned back slightly. “You appear to have very little,” he said. “That much is clear. And yet you carry something kings would value and should fetch a high price.” He paused. “Why not give it to me? And we will see that you are compensated well.”

There it was again. The easy path. The safer road. Life could be better than it was. A king before him. Power within reach. A handsome reward on the table. All it required was a shift. A change in intention. A willingness to let the journey end here.

Auðun did not look away from the king. He simply said: “I’m honored that I was able to show you such a magnificent animal, but it is not meant for you.”

Again, silence. For a long moment, Harald said nothing. Then, slowly, he smiled with something like recognition. “You are a strange man,” he said.

“If risking everything for something greater than a relegated life is strange, then, my king, I’m the strangest.”

The king’s gaze lingered, measuring. Then he gave a slight nod. “If that is how you define strange, then you’re not the only strange one in this room.”

A pause. “You have a clear vision for what your life should look like, and I believe you will see it through to the end. But know this: the road won’t be easy.”

When Auðun left that hall, the air outside felt sharper. The road ahead had changed. Not in direction. But in weight. Because at that moment there was only the vision for what the future held and what could have been had he sold the bear to the king of Norway.
